Cost Per Case Guide

How cost per lead becomes cost per signed case

Three prices show up on a lead order. They answer different questions. Mix them up and a cheap lead can be the expensive one.

Cost per lead

Cost per lead is what you pay for one submitted record that meets the criteria. It is the number on a data invoice. It does not tell you how many of those people you reached, or how many signed.

Cost per transfer

Cost per transfer is what you pay for one live connection to your intake line. You already have the conversation, so you do not apply a contact rate on top. You still need the share of those conversations that become signed cases.

Cost per signed case

Cost per signed case is what you spent divided by how many retainers you actually signed. This is the number a managing partner should compare.

For leads, signed cases equal leads times the share you reach times the share of those conversations that sign. For transfers, signed cases equal transfers times the share that sign.

A worked example

Example only. These are not KRW prices, not homepage statistics, and not a forecast of your campaign.

Say you buy 100 exclusive motor vehicle leads at an example price of 80 dollars. Spend is 8,000 dollars. You reach 60 percent of them, which is 60 conversations. Of those conversations, 20 percent sign. That is 12 signed cases. 8,000 divided by 12 is 666.67 dollars per signed case.

The same 8,000 dollars as transfers looks different. 40 live transfers at an example price of 200 dollars is 8,000 dollars. If 10 of them sign, cost per signed case is 800 dollars. The transfer costs more per unit and can still be the better or the worse buy. The signed case is the comparison.
